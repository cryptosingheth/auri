What Auri is, and is not

Auri is a non-custodial software platform. It is not a bank, not a deposit-taking institution, not a broker-dealer, and not an investment adviser. Auri does not provide banking, brokerage, investment, legal or tax advice.

Auri does not take custody of your assets. Your gold and cash balances sit in a wallet that you control, and Auri holds none of your money on its own balance sheet. Auri's role is the software you use to buy, hold, move and borrow against that asset — plus the on-ramp and off-ramp partners that connect it to the banking system in your country.

Because Auri is non-custodial, the protections that come with a bank account do not apply here. What you get instead is ownership and verifiability: you can check your holding on a public blockchain at any time, and you can move it without asking Auri's permission.

Your balances are not insured deposits

Digital assets — including tokenized gold and stablecoins — are not bank deposits. They are not covered by any government or private deposit-insurance or investor-compensation scheme. That includes the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) in Canada, the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) and the Securities Investor Protection Corporation (SIPC) in the United States, the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) in the United Kingdom, and every equivalent scheme elsewhere.

The value of gold and of crypto assets can go down as well as up. You can lose money. Nothing about gold being a long-established store of value guarantees its price will rise, or that it will not fall sharply over the period you happen to hold it.

Borrowing and liquidation risk

When you borrow against your gold, that gold becomes collateral. If its value falls far enough relative to what you owe, some or all of your gold can be sold to repay the loan. This is called liquidation. It can happen quickly, it can happen while you are asleep, and it does not require your consent — it is the condition on which the loan was made.

The level at which this happens is set by the lending market, not by Auri, and it is shown to you in the app before you confirm any borrow. Borrowing a smaller fraction of your gold's value leaves more room for the price to fall before that point is reached.

Borrow rates float with market conditions. A rate you see today is not fixed for the life of the loan.

Leverage (Boost)

Boost uses leverage to multiply your exposure to the gold price. Leverage amplifies losses exactly as hard as it amplifies gains, and it brings the liquidation point much closer. A price move that would be a mild setback on an unleveraged holding can close a leveraged position entirely.

Boost is intended for experienced users who understand this and can afford to lose the position. Only use leverage you can afford to lose.

Figures, rates and illustrations

Any rates, prices, projections or figures shown on this site — including the calculators — are illustrative only. They are not a quote, not an offer of credit, and not a promise of any particular outcome. Live figures are read from public market data and change continuously.

The actual rate, price, fee and limits that apply to you are shown in the app before you confirm a transaction. Where this site shows a figure sourced from a third party — the gold price, the borrow rate, token supply, or a lending market's size — it links to the source it read, so you can check it yourself.

Reserve and attestation figures reflect the most recent published report at the time of reading, and a market value quoted in an attestation reflects the gold price on that report's date, not today's.

Partners, regions and identity checks

Fiat deposits and withdrawals, currency conversion, and identity verification are performed by licensed third-party partners. Those services are subject to the partners' own terms, eligibility criteria and availability, and the partner used depends on where you live.

Identity verification (KYC) is required where the law of your country requires it for the on-ramp or off-ramp you choose. Funding methods differ by region — for example, Interac e-Transfer is available in Canada and not elsewhere. Feature availability varies by region and can change.

You are responsible for determining whether your use of Auri is lawful where you live, and for meeting your own tax obligations. Auri is not a tax adviser. Disposing of gold, and in some countries gifting it, can be a taxable event; you can export your full transaction history at tax time.

Third-party technology

Auri is built on public infrastructure it does not control: the Tether Gold token that represents the underlying metal, the blockchains that record it, the cross-chain mechanism that represents it on other networks, the Morpho lending markets that loans run through, and the stablecoins used for cash balances.

Each of these carries its own risk, including smart-contract defects, oracle failure, bridge failure and issuer risk. Independent security reviews reduce that risk; they do not eliminate it. A failure in any of these components could affect your funds even if Auri's own software works exactly as intended.
