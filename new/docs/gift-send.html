Gifting, sending and remitting

Gold you own can leave your account three ways: as a gift the recipient claims through a link or QR code, as a send straight to another Auri user or an Arbitrum wallet, or — once it ships — as a cross-border remittance paid out in local currency. This page covers what each does, what the person on the other end has to do, and when not to use any of them.

Gift or send: which one you want

All of them move the same asset and all require step-up verification. What differs is who the recipient is and what they must do next.

Before you start

  • You need gold already. These flows move gold you hold; they do not buy it. See Buy gold — funding is regional (Interac e-Transfer in Canada, bank transfer in the US).
  • Only free gold can move. With a loan open, part of your gold is locked as collateral and cannot be gifted, sent or sold. The app caps the amount at your free balance; to move more, repay or unlock (see Borrow against gold).
  • Step-up verification is mandatory on every gift, send and remittance — a passkey approval, or two six-digit codes (one emailed, one from your authenticator). This is separate from signing in.
  • For an @username send, get the handle exactly right — 3–20 characters, lowercase letters, numbers and underscores, set by the recipient in Profile.
  • For a wallet send, get the address from the recipient directly. Never from a message you did not expect.

Gift gold

Time: about a minute. Cost: network fee covered by Auri; see fees and limits. Reversibility: cancellable any time before the recipient claims.

  1. Open Gift gold from the Actions catalogue or the move-money menu, under Move gold.
  2. Choose delivery. Contact sends the claim link for you, by mobile number or email — the mobile field offers dial codes for Canada, the US, India, the UK, the UAE, Singapore, Australia, the Philippines, Pakistan, Nigeria, Germany and France. Share link and QR code hand the claim back to you, so you can deliver it in a card, over a messaging app, or on a screen in person.
  3. Enter the amount in dollars, grams, ounces or XAUT, with the live equivalent shown in the other units. Presets are $50 / $100 / $250 / $500, or 1 / 5 / 10 / 25 g, or 0.1 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 1 oz. Capped at your free gold.
  4. Pick an occasion — Birthday, Wedding, Festival, Shagun, Congrats, or Just because. This is presentation, not mechanics: it changes what the recipient sees on opening the claim.
  5. Add a message. Optional, and the part people actually remember.
  6. Complete step-up verification.
  7. Check the review screen: gift value, the amount in gold (oz), delivery method, occasion. The gold figure is the one that matters — that is what the recipient ends up owning, whatever the price does later.
  8. Confirm. The gold moves into gift escrow on Arbitrum and a claim link is generated. Contact gifts confirm the link is on its way; link and QR gifts show the claim URL with Copy, WhatsApp and Email share.

What you'll see when it works

A success screen naming the amount in ounces, plus the claim URL for link and QR gifts. The gift then appears in Activity with a Pending claim badge, and Home shows a banner counting gifts awaiting claim. Nothing is the recipient's yet — the gold is escrowed until they claim it.

How the recipient claims

They open the link or scan the QR, create an Auri account if needed, and the gold moves from escrow into their vault. From then it is theirs: hold, sell, send onward, borrow against, or move to their own wallet. You cannot take it back.

Tell the recipient it is coming before you send it. An unannounced message containing a gold claim link looks exactly like a scam, and that is the most common reason gifts sit unclaimed.

Claiming means signing up: passwordless sign-in, and biometrics on mobile. Auri's identity check is triggered by funding and cash-out rails — bank transfer, debit card, Interac — so claiming a gift and holding the gold does not by itself require ID. The moment they want to sell and withdraw cash, it will.

Unclaimed gifts, reminders and cancelling

Until claimed, the gold sits in escrow: out of your spendable balance, not yet the recipient's. In Activity, a pending gift states that the recipient has not claimed it and that the gold stays on hold until they do. Two actions sit on that row:

  • Remind / re-send — re-delivers the claim to the same contact.
  • Cancel gift — pulls the gold out of escrow back to your free balance, confirmed as the gift cancelled and funds returned.

Auri's V2.2 prototype states no expiry period for an unclaimed gift and no automatic refund after a set number of days. Until that is published, treat an unclaimed gift as something you must chase or cancel yourself. Escrowed value still tracks the gold price while it waits — it is not parked in dollars.

Send gold to a person or a wallet

Time: immediate to an @username. Cost: network fee covered; see fees and limits. Reversibility: a wallet send is irreversible on confirmation.

  1. Open Send gold and choose Contact, @Username, or Wallet.
  2. Enter the destination. Contact takes a mobile number (same dial-code picker) or an email. @Username delivers as gold immediately with no claim step. Wallet takes an Arbitrum address and shows an irreversibility warning worth reading.
  3. Enter the amount in dollars, grams, ounces or XAUT. Presets $50 / $100 / $250 / $500, capped at your free gold.
  4. Complete step-up verification.
  5. Check the review screen: amount, gold equivalent, destination, and method — On-chain wallet or Claim as gold. If it says Claim as gold, the recipient must claim it exactly like a gift.
  6. Confirm.

A wallet send cannot be undone, and Auri cannot recover it. The asset that arrives is XAUt0 on Arbitrum One — the LayerZero version of Tether Gold, contract 0x40461291347e1eCbb09499F3371D3f17f10d7159. The receiving wallet must support Arbitrum One and be able to display that token, or the balance looks missing even though the transfer succeeded. Send a small test amount to any new address first. A typo, the wrong network, or an exchange deposit address that does not accept XAUt0 all mean the gold is gone.

Cross-border remittance

Coming soon — you cannot do this today. Remittance is modelled in Auri's V2.2 prototype but is not in the live product. What follows describes the intended flow; do not plan a real payment around it.

The design converts gold or your cash balance into a local-currency payout: pick a destination country, choose to fund from gold or cash, enter an amount, and a live panel shows what the recipient receives, the rate, the fee and an expected arrival time. Then payout details, step-up verification, review, and a processing state. The stated fee is 0.7% of the amount sent, with mid-market rates and no FX markup claimed — that second part is the substantive one, because remittance cost normally hides in the FX margin, not the visible fee. For scale, the World Bank puts the global average cost of sending $200 at 6.49% in Q1 2025 (banks 9.50%, digital providers 3.65%). Judge any number all-in.

The receipt breaks the transfer into auditable legs: gold sold to USDC on Uniswap v3, USDC converted to local currency, then payout over local rails. So funding from gold means selling gold — you give up the exposure, and the disposal has tax consequences. Remittance is also the one flow that always passes through a processing state, because a real bank with real cut-offs sits on the other side: a transfer started late on a Friday can land after the weekend however fast the on-chain legs are.

Why gift gold instead of a gift card

A gift card is a liability of a merchant: dollar-denominated, usable in one place, quietly eroded by inflation, and frequently never fully spent. A gold gift is different in kind — the recipient owns a claim on a specific quantity of vaulted metal, sellable anywhere Auri operates, with a price unrelated to any company's solvency. Five grams is five grams in ten years. Five grams is not a promise from a retailer.

The unit matters more than people expect. Grams read as a gift; dollars read as a transfer. "Ten grams for your wedding" is an object; a dollar figure is a bank notification. That is why you can type the amount in grams or ounces directly, and why a round weight usually lands better than a round price.

Occasions where it works

The built-in occasions track where gold gifting is already a norm rather than a novelty: weddings, births, milestone birthdays, and festival gifting — Shagun and Diwali in South Asian households, where giving gold at an auspicious time is the tradition itself. It also suits the graduation or first-job gift where you specifically do not want the money spent this month. Gold is illiquid in the useful sense: claiming is easy, spending takes a deliberate decision.

What the recipient is taking on

Be honest that you are signing someone up for an app. If they ever want cash rather than gold, they will go through an identity check and a bank connection. For a confident adult that is a short errand; for an older relative it may be a phone call with you walking them through it — budget for that call, or hand over the QR code in person and do it together. Set price expectations too: gold pays no dividend and no interest, it has had long stretches of falling real value, and a gift given at a high price can be worth less a year later. If a fixed sum on a fixed date is the point, gift cash.

Gifting to a child is the one case with no documented answer: Auri publishes nothing on custodial or minor accounts, so how a gift to a minor is held, and who controls it, is UNKNOWN. In practice a gift claimed into an adult's account is owned by that adult. Do not assume otherwise until Auri says so.

When not to gift or send gold

  • They need to spend the money now. They would have to claim, sell (0.49% Auri fee on the sale) and withdraw, then wait on a bank transfer.
  • The gold is collateral. Locked gold cannot move, and freeing it means repaying. Do not unwind a loan to fund a present.
  • You would have to sell at a bad moment to fund it. A gift is not a reason to realise a loss.
  • They cannot or will not use an app. The gold sits in escrow and the gesture never lands.
  • Someone contacted you unprompted asking for it. An urgent request for a claim link or a wallet transfer is the shape of a scam. Gold sent to a wallet is unrecoverable, which is precisely why fraudsters ask for it.

Tax and reporting

Giving gold away is still a disposal of an asset in many tax systems, even though no cash reached you. Depending on where you live, a gift can trigger a capital gain measured from what you paid to the value on the day you gave it; some countries tax or require reporting of gifts above a threshold, on the giver's side, the recipient's side, or both; and treatment differs again for gifts to a spouse, to a minor, or across a border. Funding a remittance from gold is unambiguously a sale.

Profile includes a Tax & reports section with transaction-history export and realised gain/loss figures — the raw material an accountant will ask for. The obligation is yours. Thresholds and treatment vary by country and change; check your own jurisdiction and get a qualified adviser if a gift is large.

What can go wrong

The recipient hasn't claimed the gift

Open Activity, find the row badged Pending claim, then use Remind / re-send or Cancel gift to return the gold. Message the person yourself first — most unclaimed gifts are unclaimed because the link looked untrustworthy.

Only part of my gold is free to move

You have a loan open and the rest is locked as collateral. Repay, or unlock a specific amount of collateral, then retry. See Borrow against gold.

I sent gold to the wrong wallet address

There is no recovery — Auri cannot reverse an on-chain transfer and neither can anyone else. If the address belongs to an exchange or a wallet you can contact, that is your only avenue and it usually fails. Hence the small test transfer to any new address.

The @username wasn't found

Handles are 3–20 characters, lowercase letters, numbers and underscores, and the recipient must have set one in Profile. Confirm the spelling, or send to their mobile number or email and let them claim.

I can't find remittance in the app

It is not released. Nothing in the current app sends a local-currency payout abroad.
