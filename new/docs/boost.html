Boost borrows US dollars against gold you already own, buys more gold with them, and pledges that gold too — looping until your exposure reaches the multiplier you picked. It multiplies gains and losses by the same factor, and adds a failure mode plain gold holding does not have: forced closure.

Read this first. At 3× exposure a 10% fall in gold costs you 30% of your money, and a 13% fall ends the position — you do not get to wait for the recovery. Boost is for experienced users who can afford to lose everything they commit. If losing it would hurt, do not use it.

At a glance: two minutes to open · interest accrues daily on the borrowed amount at a floating rate · confirming is irreversible, though you can close early at the price gold is at then · off by default.

What Boost actually is

The app's How leverage works panel gives three steps: borrow USDC against the gold you hold, buy more gold with it, pledge that gold for the next borrow — repeated to your multiplier. More gold means bigger gains, and bigger losses if gold falls.

The slider runs 1.25× to 5× in 0.25 steps, with chips at 2×, 3×, 4× and 5×. The rest is fixed arithmetic: at multiplier m your loan-to-value is (m − 1) / m — 2× is 50%, 3× is 67%, 4× is 75%, 5× is 80%. Your commit is the only equity; everything above it is debt.

Why people do it: they already wanted more gold at today's price and were short of cash. That is the whole case — a conviction trade, not a savings strategy. What you keep: title to all the gold. What you owe: the USDC plus interest, growing daily whatever gold does. What you could lose: the entire commit.

Start with the loss, not the gain

One position, both directions, identical numbers. You commit US$10,000 of free gold at 3×. Auri borrows US$20,000 and buys more gold, so you hold US$30,000 of gold and owe US$20,000 at a 67% LTV.

  • Gold falls 10%. Your gold is worth US$27,000; the debt is still US$20,000, because debt does not fall with the price. Equity is US$7,000 — you have lost US$3,000, 30% of your money, and your LTV is now 74%.
  • Gold rises 10%. Gold US$33,000, debt US$20,000, equity US$13,000 — up US$3,000, or 30%.
  • Unlevered. The same US$10,000 and the same 10% fall costs US$1,000.

Before financing the two directions are symmetric; financing is not, since you pay it either way. And the loss column hides something: that 10% fall leaves you 3.8% from closure. The table is the same ±10% frame the Boost screen shows — read the loss column first.

Every multiplier from about 4.35× upward gives an LTV at or above the market's liquidation level, so 4.5× and 5× are not usable positions — they start at the line. The app blocks you only at a resulting LTV of 82%, which the 5× cap never quite hits. That block is not what keeps you safe.

Liquidation: the level, the buffer, which number to believe

Boost borrows in a public market — USDC against XAUt0 on Morpho, Arbitrum One. Its liquidation loan-to-value is 77%, written on chain and readable by anyone on the market page. Reach it and a liquidator repays part of your loan and takes gold for it: no phone call, no grace period, nothing to negotiate. As a price, at 3× with gold near US$4,376/oz that level sits around US$3,789/oz. Write your own number down before you open anything, and check the live one in the app.

The Boost screen is more flattering than the market. Its "Gold can fall X%" safety buffer, its liquidation price and its health-factor dial are all calculated against a liquidation level above 77%. The buffer it prints is wider than you have, and the liquidation price it prints sits below the price that will actually close you. Plan against 77%, treat a Boost health factor near 1.1 as the line rather than 1.0, and read the buffer as the optimistic end of a range. We are reconciling this in the product.

The forced exit. Gold drifts down 10%, your LTV goes 67% → 74%, a loan-health alert fires. Gold slips another 4%, the LTV crosses 77%, and a liquidator clears part of your debt with your gold at whatever price gold is at — realised whether you were watching or not. Any equity left is still yours, but if gold rallies next week you are no longer in the position to benefit. The liquidator is also paid an incentive out of the position, so liquidation costs more than the arithmetic above; the exact penalty here is UNKNOWN.

What it costs to hold

You pay interest on the borrowed portion — (m − 1) × commit — for as long as the position is open, and that rate floats with supply and demand in the lending market. When we last checked it was near 3.5% and had already moved within the hour. Never plan on a fixed number; the live APR is shown in the app and on the site.

On the 3× example, US$20,000 near 3.5% is roughly US$58 a month — about 7% a year against the US$10,000 you committed, which is the number that matters. Go sideways for a year at 3× and you are down roughly 7% with a narrower buffer, because the debt grew and the collateral did not. Leverage decays in a flat market. Before you start

  • Pro mode on. Boost does not exist in the app until you enable Pro mode in Profile: "leverage your gold for more exposure. For experienced users."
  • No standard loan open. The two are mutually exclusive, and the consequence is easy to miss: while boosted, you cannot borrow cash. If you might need a draw, take the standard loan instead.
  • Free gold to commit. Only unpledged gold qualifies; chips are offered at $500 / $1,000 / $2,500 / max free.
  • Accept that the gold is unspendable. It is all collateral — no selling, gifting, sending or remitting until you close.
  • A loss number, decided in advance. Before you touch the slider, not after.

Open a position

  1. Open Boost. With Pro mode on it appears in the navigation and in Actions. Pick the Boost tab; Standard loan beside it is the unlevered product.
  2. Enter your commit and set the multiplier. The commit is your equity and the most you can lose. As you drag the slider, watch the LTV and liquidation price move, not just the exposure figure.
  3. Read the scenario panel as a loss. It shows a ±10% gold move against the unlevered outcome. Look at the down case and ask whether you would hold through it.
  4. Check the review screen, then confirm. Multiplier, gold price, commit, exposure, cash borrowed, new LTV, liquidation price. Convert the gap between today's price and the liquidation price into a percentage yourself. On confirm, the debt starts accruing and the liquidation level is live.

Manage and exit

The open-position panel shows commit, exposure, cash borrowed, entry price, current price, liquidation price and unrealised P&L. Watch the gap between current and liquidation price, not the profit line. You can close 25%, 50% or 75% — the panel shows the gold freed and loan repaid — or close entirely, which sells enough gold to clear the debt and returns the rest as free gold. Partial closing is the deleveraging lever: it lowers LTV and widens the buffer.

The panel offers reducing, not reinforcing. There is no "add more gold" button, so in a falling market your only lever is to shrink the position — and shrinking realises the loss on the part you close. Whether adding collateral will be supported is UNKNOWN. Plan your exit assuming reducing is all you get.

When not to use Boost

  • If losing the commit would hurt. Not annoy — hurt. Assume the whole thing can go.
  • If you cannot check weekly at minimum. Gold trades continuously and there is no margin call to prompt you.
  • If the multiplier is what makes the trade interesting. Boost only makes sense if you wanted the gold anyway.
  • If the price just ran and you feel late. That is when leverage is most expensive and the buffer most likely to be tested.
  • Alongside a recurring buy. Auto-Invest survives drawdowns by buying through them; leverage removes you from the market during one.
  • For rewards. Borrowing earns Jewels. Jewels are a rounding error against a liquidation.

Size it backwards from the loss

Start from the loss you can absorb: commit = tolerable loss ÷ (multiplier × the fall you accept as plausible). If you can absorb US$2,000 and treat a 15% fall as plausible, at 2× your commit is US$6,667 — not "whatever is free in my vault". The same tolerance at 4× allows only US$3,333, and buys a buffer under 3%. Higher multipliers don't let you commit more; they force you to commit less. The floor: equity is gone entirely on a fall of 1 / m — 50% at 2×, 33% at 3×, 20% at 5×. Liquidation always arrives first, which is what stops you owing more than you put in.

What can go wrong

  • "Boost isn't in my navigation." Pro mode is off. Turn it on in Profile.
  • "It says to repay my loan first." A standard loan is open. Repay it in full, or use that instead.
  • "My commit is capped below my gold balance." The rest is pledged; only free gold counts. See locked versus free gold.
  • "I was closed out without being asked." That is liquidation working as designed — in the lending market at 77% LTV, not at Auri's discretion, and it does not wait for you.
  • "I closed part of it and my loss got bigger." Closing realises the loss on that portion. It reduces risk; it cannot undo a fall.

Related
