Auri user docs

Auri turns money into real allocated gold that you own, then lets that gold do work: borrow cash against it without selling, send it to family, or buy a little every week automatically. These pages document what the app does today, number by number — what each action costs, what it changes, what can go wrong, and when not to do it at all.

What Auri is, in 60 seconds

When you buy, your money becomes USDC on the Arbitrum network and is swapped for tokenized gold. The gold is Tether Gold; on Arbitrum the token you hold is XAUt0 (0x40461291347e1eCbb09499F3371D3f17f10d7159), backed one-for-one by native XAUt on Ethereum, itself backed by one troy ounce of vaulted physical gold per token. It lands in a self-custody vault whose address you can see in your profile. Amounts display in your chosen currency; settlement is in USDC.

Once you hold gold you can borrow dollars against it instead of selling. Auri posts your gold as collateral in a public lending market on Morpho (Arbitrum One) and hands you USDC. Nothing is sold, and anyone can inspect that market at app.morpho.org. The rate floats: that market sets it minute to minute, not Auri.

Auri charges 0.49% to buy gold and 0.49% to sell, and covers the network fee, so you never need a gas token. Every other fee, minimum and limit lives in Reference.

Not live yet. The Auri card is a waitlist, not a product — you can join the queue, you cannot spend gold today. Cross-border remittance is also not live. Wherever these appear they are labelled Coming soon, and no page gives you steps for them.

Is Auri right for you?

Gold has no cash flow: no dividend, no interest, no earnings. It pays you only if someone later pays more than you did, so the question is not "what will this return" but "what do I want to hold instead of cash". Three patterns cover most people here.

You want savings that aren't entirely in your own currency

Read Start here, then Buy gold, then set a small recurring buy in Auto-Invest — from as little as $10 a cycle. Read Trust and proof before the balance gets large.

You need cash and don't want to sell

Selling ends your gold position and may create a taxable disposal; borrowing keeps the position and creates an obligation instead. Read Borrowing strategies first, then Borrow against your gold and Repay.

You want to put gold in someone else's hands

Gold is a durable gift rather than money that gets spent, and grams read as a gift where a dollar figure reads as a transfer. See Gift and send gold.

When Auri is the wrong tool

  • You are carrying high-interest debt. Paying it down beats any expected gold return, and it is certain.
  • You need the money within twelve months. Gold can be down when your deadline arrives, and exiting costs 0.49%.
  • You are buying because the price just went up.
  • You want to borrow for something your income cannot repay, or you cannot check your position weekly. See Risks.

Everyday actions

The four things most people do in a normal month. Each page states cost, time and reversibility before the steps.

  • Buy gold — buy from your cash balance or a funding rail, in dollars, grams, troy ounces or XAUT, and read the confirmation screen well enough to catch a bad one.
  • Add and withdraw cash — fund your account and take money out: bank transfer in the US, Interac e-Transfer in Canada, debit card, or USDC from a wallet you control.
  • Gift and send gold — send gold by username, to a wallet address, or as a claim link to someone with no account, and manage a gift nobody has claimed.
  • Auto-Invest — run a daily, weekly or monthly recurring buy, pause, resize or stop it, and see what a recurring buy does not protect you from.

The constraint that surprises people. Once you borrow, part of your gold becomes locked collateral, and locked gold cannot be sold, gifted or sent until you repay or unlock it. The app tracks free and locked balances separately and blocks outbound actions above the free amount: locked versus free gold.

Borrowing

Borrowing is the part of Auri where you can lose gold you already own, so it gets the most detailed treatment.

  • Borrow against your gold — take a loan in USDC, size it deliberately instead of accepting the maximum, and read your loan-to-value, health factor and liquidation price.
  • Repay and unlock — repay from cash, watch your loan-to-value fall, and unlock collateral so gold is free to move again. Unlocking is its own action, not an automatic consequence of repaying.

Read this before you borrow: Borrowing strategies — borrow or sell, how to choose a loan-to-value and why the maximum on offer is never the right answer, what your buffer is worth in dollars and percent, the three ways out of a loan, and when not to borrow at all.

Liquidation, plainly. The lending market Auri uses has an on-chain liquidation loan-to-value of 77% — a line to stay well clear of, not a target. If gold falls far enough for your loan to reach it, part of your gold is sold to repay the loan, and you do not choose the moment. Your liquidation price, health factor and buffer are shown live in the app: how liquidation works.

Advanced

  • Boost (leverage) — borrow against gold, buy more gold with the proceeds, repeat to a chosen multiplier, then manage or partly close the position. Shows the amplified gain and loss on identical numbers, and how much closer liquidation moves at each multiplier.

Boost is gated, and should be. It appears only after you switch on Pro mode, cannot be open alongside a standard loan, and refuses multipliers that push your loan-to-value too near liquidation. Leverage magnifies losses as reliably as gains, and costs a floating rate to hold even in a flat market. If you have not read how liquidation works, you are not ready for it.

Trust and safety

For a product that converts savings into metal you will never touch, "where is it and how do I check" comes before "how do I buy".

  • Trust, custody and proof — follow the chain from your token to a physical bar, check the live lending market yourself, and understand what the published attestations do and do not prove.
  • Risks — the full list in plain language: price risk, liquidation, floating rates, smart-contract and bridge risk, custody risk, irreversible transfers, and the risks created by your own devices.

Run the numbers before you commit

The landing page carries two calculators that use the same arithmetic as the app: how much gold an amount buys, and — from your gold value and loan size — your loan-to-value, monthly interest, liquidation price and how far gold must fall to reach it. Both read the live gold price and the live borrow rate, not a stored number.

Try it: the buy and borrow calculators. Move the loan slider until the liquidation price stops feeling comfortable — that number, not the maximum you are allowed to borrow, is how to size a loan. It is an illustration, not a quote; your actual rate, limits and eligibility appear in the app before you confirm.

How to read these pages

  • Live numbers beat documented ones. These pages explain the model; the app shows the live value for your position. Where they differ, trust the app and tell us the page is stale.
  • Money examples are in US dollars unless a rail is genuinely regional — Interac e-Transfer is Canada-only. Gold shows as dollars, grams, troy ounces or XAUT, and you can type an amount in any of them; one troy ounce is 31.1035 grams.
  • Outbound money gets a second check. Gifts, sends and anything leaving to an external wallet need a step-up approval after the review screen — a passkey, or two six-digit codes. Auri cannot approve it for you, and on-chain transfers cannot be reversed by anyone.

Not advice

Nothing here is financial, tax or legal advice, and none of it recommends buying gold, borrowing or using leverage. Gold can fall in value, including for years at a stretch. Borrowing against it can cost you part of your gold through liquidation, at a moment you do not choose. If the amounts matter to you, take advice from someone who knows your circumstances.
